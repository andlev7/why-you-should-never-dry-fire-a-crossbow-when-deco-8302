Why You Should Never Dry-Fire a Crossbow When Decocking, and the Safe Way to Do It

If you're a crossbow enthusiast, you've probably heard the golden rule: never dry-fire your crossbow. But do you know why this matters so much when you're decocking? Many hunters and sport shooters treat decocking as a simple end-of-day routine, but the method you use can either preserve your crossbow's longevity or cause serious, irreversible damage. Let's break down why dry-firing during decocking is dangerous and what you should be doing instead.

The Danger of Dry-Firing Your Crossbow

A crossbow operates using stored mechanical energy in its limbs. When you fire a bolt, that energy is transferred through the string and into the projectile. The limbs then return to their resting position. This rapid release and return is designed to happen when a bolt is in the flight path—the bolt acts as a buffer, absorbing energy and protecting the internal mechanisms.

When you dry-fire (release the string without a bolt loaded), something catastrophic happens: all that energy has nowhere to go. Instead of being transferred to a projectile, the full force bounces back through the crossbow's internal components. The string, limbs, rail, and trigger mechanism experience extreme stress they're not designed to handle.

Over time, dry-firing can cause:

  • Limb fractures or splinters
  • String separation or fraying
  • Rail damage and misalignment
  • Trigger mechanism failure
  • Loss of shooting accuracy
  • Costly repairs or complete crossbow replacement

The worst part? Many of these damages aren't immediately visible. A crossbow might seem fine after a dry-fire, but internal stress fractures could be developing, waiting to cause catastrophic failure when you least expect it.

Why Decocking Matters

Decocking is the process of safely releasing the tension from your crossbow's limbs without firing a bolt. It's an essential practice for crossbow safety and longevity. You should decock your crossbow when:

  • You're finished hunting or practicing for the day
  • You need to transport your crossbow
  • You're storing your crossbow for an extended period
  • You need to perform maintenance

Leaving a crossbow cocked for extended periods puts continuous stress on the limbs and internal components, causing them to lose their elasticity and power over time. This is why decocking is so important—it relieves that stress and keeps your crossbow in top condition.

However, the method you use to decock makes all the difference. For specific guidance on safely decocking an Excalibur crossbow, always consult your manufacturer's instructions, as different models may have slightly different procedures.

The Safe Way to Decock Your Crossbow

The safest way to decock your crossbow is to fire a bolt into a safe target. This might sound counterintuitive, but it's the method endorsed by virtually every crossbow manufacturer. Here's why it works:

  • Controlled energy release: The bolt safely absorbs and carries away the stored energy, preventing internal damage.
  • Designed function: Your crossbow is built to operate this way—firing with a bolt in place.
  • No stress on components: All mechanisms work as intended, with no unusual strain.

The proper procedure:

  1. Find a safe shooting area with a proper target or backstop
  2. Load a bolt into your crossbow
  3. Aim at your target
  4. Fire normally
  5. Your crossbow is now decocked and safe to store

Never use the trigger to decock without a bolt, and never attempt mechanical methods to force the limbs down. These shortcuts might save you five minutes, but they'll cost you hundreds in repairs.

Conclusion

Decocking your crossbow properly is a small investment of time that pays massive dividends in equipment longevity and performance. Always fire a bolt into a safe target to decock, never dry-fire, and follow your manufacturer's specific instructions. Your crossbow will thank you with years of reliable, accurate shooting.
